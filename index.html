Method and sources

How performance is measured

  • Margin is the candidate's share minus the main opponent's share, as a percentage of all votes in the race, from the candidate's own point of view.
  • PVI-implied margin is 2 × PVI in the candidate's party's direction (D+5 → Democrat +10, Republican −10). Cook PVI measures two-party lean relative to the nation, so this baseline assumes an evenly split national environment. In a year that leans toward one party, most of that party's candidates will "overperform" by a similar amount, so compare candidates against each other, not against zero.
  • National environment is the median amount by which Democrats ran ahead of their PVI-implied margin, across every race with a Democrat-versus-Republican comparison. It is currently and is recomputed whenever results refresh. The default baseline, "PVI + environment", shifts each PVI-implied margin by that amount toward the party the year favored, so a candidate on the diagonal did exactly as well as a typical candidate of their party. "PVI only" shows the unshifted baseline.
  • vs. 2024 is the candidate's margin minus Harris's (for Democrats) or Trump's (for Republicans) 2024 margin in the same state or district.
  • Where a party fielded no candidate and an independent was the main opponent (for example Nebraska and Idaho Senate), the independent is used as that side. Same-party contests (California top-two, uncontested seats) have no D-vs-R comparison and are listed with blank values.

Caveats

  • Results are unofficial until each state certifies; races marked "Certified" use certified totals entered by hand. A handful of House races were still being counted when this snapshot was taken (see "Race call").
  • District 2024 margins are left out for the nine states that used new House maps in 2026 (AL, CA, FL, LA, NC, OH, TN, TX, UT), because the 2024 figures are for the old lines. Missouri's new map was blocked, so Missouri uses its 2022 map and its PVI and 2024 figures match those lines. NY-21's 2024 district figure is blank in the source.
  • Statewide 2024 margins are summed from the district file.
  • The results source lists only the top two candidates in each race, so shares and margins are taken against the race's full vote count, and votes for anyone else are not attributed to a party. Louisiana and Alaska let several candidates from one party share the November ballot; where more than 10% of the vote there went to unlisted candidates, the top Democrat against the top Republican is not a party-against-party margin, and the race has no comparison.
